What to include in a technical report

  • The page URL where you saw the problem or statement.
  • Your CPU, GPU, RAM, resolution and workload (gaming, creative, office or mixed).
  • What result the calculator gave and what you expected instead.
  • Any evidence: a link to a review, a spec sheet, a benchmark or a monitoring screenshot.

Specific, reproducible reports are the easiest to check and act on.

What you can contact us about

  • Calculator results: a score that looks unrealistic for a given CPU and GPU pairing.
  • Missing hardware: a processor or graphics card you would like added to the lists.
  • Corrections: a factual or technical error in a guide.
  • Topic suggestions: a PC performance question you would like a guide to answer.
  • Privacy and accessibility: questions about our Privacy Policy or problems using the site.
  • Partnerships: editorial or tool collaborations relevant to PC performance.

Before you write

Many questions are answered in our guides. If you are unsure why a result looks the way it does, read how to check a PC bottleneck and the calculator methodology. For help with a specific slowdown, try our stuttering guide or the 100% CPU usage guide.
