PC guides

Common questions.

What is a PC bottleneck?

A bottleneck is a component or condition that limits a particular workload before the rest of the system. The limiting component can change with resolution, settings, frame rate and application.

Is a small bottleneck bad?

No. Every system has a limiting factor in a particular workload. A small imbalance only matters if it prevents you from reaching your intended experience.

Does resolution change a bottleneck?

Yes. Higher resolutions generally increase GPU rendering work, while high-refresh 1080p gaming can put relatively more pressure on the CPU.

How can I verify the result?

Monitor GPU utilization, CPU usage per core, clocks, memory use, temperatures and frame time while running the exact game or application you care about.

How to use the calculator

Check your PC bottleneck before changing hardware

A PC bottleneck is not a permanent property of a computer. The component that limits performance can change with the game, application, resolution, graphics settings, frame-rate target and background workload. That is why a useful bottleneck check should be treated as a starting point rather than a single percentage that applies to every situation.

Start by selecting the processor and graphics card that are installed in your system. Choose the resolution you actually play or work at, then select the workload that most closely matches your use. Add your RAM capacity, storage type, peak temperature and PSU wattage so the supporting-hardware checks have context.

How to verify a calculator result

After running the calculator, reproduce the workload on your real PC and monitor GPU utilization, CPU usage by core, clock speeds, RAM usage, temperatures and frame time. A GPU that remains close to full utilization during a graphics-heavy game can be behaving exactly as intended. Conversely, a low GPU utilization combined with one or more CPU cores near their limit can point toward a CPU-side limitation. Stutter can also come from RAM pressure, thermal throttling, storage activity, drivers or background software.

Why resolution matters

Resolution changes how much work the graphics card performs for every frame. At 1080p and very high refresh rates, a fast GPU may be able to render frames quickly enough that the CPU becomes the limiting factor. Moving to 1440p or 4K generally increases GPU workload and can change the balance. This is why a CPU and GPU pairing should always be evaluated in the context of the display and settings you intend to use.

Why the result is an estimate

This tool uses relative component values and workload adjustments rather than a database of measured FPS for every game. It cannot reproduce every driver version, game engine, BIOS setting, cooling condition or background process. Use the report to identify questions worth testing, then confirm important decisions with current reviews and real-world benchmarks.
