The Ultimate PC Bottleneck and Upgrade Checklist: What to Check Before You Buy

By Muhammad Hanfi, founder and editor

Upgrading a PC can be expensive. Unfortunately, thousands of gamers waste money every day buying new graphics cards or processors that they do not actually need. Before you spend a single dollar, use this comprehensive checklist to diagnose your exact bottleneck.

Why You Must Diagnose Before You Buy

If your car is driving slowly, you don't blindly buy new tires without checking the engine, the transmission, and the fuel first. A PC is a system of interconnected parts. Upgrading a component that is not the bottleneck will yield zero performance increase.

For example, if your RAM is full, causing your game to stutter, buying a $1,000 RTX 4080 graphics card will not fix the stuttering. The RAM is still full. The $1,000 was wasted.

To avoid this, you must run a diagnostic test. Download a free hardware monitoring tool like MSI Afterburner (with RivaTuner) or use the built-in Xbox Game Bar. Play your most demanding game for 15 minutes, look at the numbers, and run through the checklist below.

The Pre-Upgrade Diagnostic Checklist

1. The GPU Bottleneck Check (Are you already getting max performance?)

The first thing to check is your GPU usage percentage.

  • Is your GPU Usage consistently at 95% to 100%?

Diagnosis: You have a GPU bottleneck. This is actually exactly what you want! It means your CPU, RAM, and motherboard are doing a great job feeding the graphics card, and you are getting every single frame you paid for out of your GPU.

Upgrade Decision: If your GPU is at 100% but your FPS is still too low for your liking, you must upgrade the GPU. Upgrading the CPU in this scenario will do absolutely nothing.

2. The CPU Bottleneck Check (Is the processor holding you back?)

If your GPU usage is significantly below 95% (e.g., hovering around 60% or 70%), something else in the system is holding it back. It is time to check the processor.

  • Is your overall CPU Usage above 85%?
  • More importantly: Are one or two individual CPU cores hitting 95-100%?

Diagnosis: You have a CPU bottleneck. The processor cannot calculate game logic and send draw calls fast enough to keep the graphics card busy. The GPU is literally sitting idle waiting for instructions.

Upgrade Decision: Do not buy a faster GPU. A faster GPU will only make the bottleneck worse. You must upgrade the CPU. Note that upgrading the CPU often requires buying a new motherboard and new RAM to match the new processor generation.

3. The RAM Capacity Check (Are you spilling over?)

If neither the GPU nor the individual CPU cores are hitting 100%, but the game is still stuttering horribly, you must check system memory.

  • Is your System RAM usage at 90% or higher of your total capacity? (e.g., using 14.5GB out of 16GB)

Diagnosis: You have a RAM capacity bottleneck. Windows is frantically trying to manage a full memory pool by dumping data onto your slow storage drive, causing massive frame-time spikes and stuttering.

Upgrade Decision: Upgrade to a larger capacity RAM kit (e.g., moving from 16GB to 32GB). Ensure you install them in dual-channel configuration and enable XMP/EXPO in the BIOS.

4. The VRAM Check (Is the graphics card out of memory?)

This is different from system RAM. This is the memory built directly into your graphics card.

  • Is your Dedicated GPU Memory (VRAM) usage hitting the maximum limit of your card? (e.g., using 7.9GB out of an 8GB card).

Diagnosis: The game's textures and resolution require more memory than your card physically possesses. The game has to pull texture data from system RAM over the PCIe bus, resulting in massive stuttering, especially when turning the camera quickly.

Upgrade Decision: First, try lowering the "Texture Quality" setting in the game or dropping the resolution. If you refuse to lower settings, you must buy a new graphics card with more VRAM (e.g., moving from an 8GB card to a 12GB or 16GB card).

5. The Thermal Throttling Check (Is your PC protecting itself?)

Sometimes the hardware is perfectly capable, but it is too hot to run at full speed.

  • Is your CPU Temperature consistently above 90°C?
  • Is your GPU Temperature consistently above 85°C (or Hotspot above 105°C)?

Diagnosis: You are experiencing thermal throttling. To prevent melting, your CPU or GPU is artificially slowing itself down. You don't have a hardware limitation; you have a cooling limitation.

Upgrade Decision: Do not buy a new CPU or GPU. Fix the thermals. Clean the dust out of your PC, buy a better CPU cooler, apply fresh thermal paste, or add intake fans to your PC case. This will restore your lost performance instantly.

The Hidden Compatibility Checklist

If you have run the diagnostics above and decided to buy a new component, you must check these final compatibility hurdles before hitting "Purchase":

  1. Power Supply (PSU): If you are buying a massive new GPU, check its wattage requirement. Do you have a powerful enough power supply? Does your PSU have the correct PCIe cables?
  2. Physical Case Clearance: Modern GPUs (like the RTX 4080 or 4090) are gigantic. Get a tape measure and verify the card will physically fit inside your PC case without hitting the front fans.
  3. Motherboard BIOS: If you are upgrading your CPU on the same motherboard (e.g., moving from a Ryzen 3600 to a Ryzen 5800X3D), you almost certainly need to update your motherboard BIOS before removing the old CPU, otherwise the PC will not boot.
  4. PCIe Bottlenecks: If you are buying a budget GPU with only 8 PCIe lanes (like an RTX 4060 or RX 7600), putting it into an older PCIe Gen 3 motherboard will cause a slight performance loss.

Conclusion

An informed upgrade is a cost-effective upgrade. By taking 15 minutes to monitor your CPU usage, GPU usage, temperatures, and RAM, you guarantee that the money you spend will actually result in higher frame rates and smoother gameplay.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I upgrade my CPU or GPU first?

You should only upgrade the component that is currently acting as your primary bottleneck. If your GPU usage is constantly at 100%, upgrade the GPU. If your GPU usage is low, but your CPU cores are maxed out, you must upgrade your CPU to see any performance gains.

Will a better CPU increase my FPS if I am GPU bottlenecked?

No. If your graphics card is already working at 100% capacity, buying a faster processor will not increase your average FPS at all, because the GPU simply cannot draw frames any faster than it already is.

How do I check my PC bottleneck before upgrading?

Before buying anything, use a monitoring tool like MSI Afterburner while playing your heaviest game. Record your GPU usage, per-core CPU usage, system RAM usage, and temperatures. Compare these numbers against our upgrade checklist to isolate the real problem.

Do I need to reinstall Windows after upgrading my PC?

If you upgrade your GPU, you should use a tool like DDU (Display Driver Uninstaller) to remove the old drivers before installing the new ones, but Windows can stay. If you upgrade your motherboard and CPU, it is highly recommended to do a fresh reinstall of Windows to prevent deep-rooted driver conflicts and stuttering.
