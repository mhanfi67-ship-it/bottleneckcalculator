How to Fix 100% CPU Usage in Games Without Immediately Upgrading

By Muhammad Hanfi, founder and editor

Hitting 100% CPU usage while gaming results in severe stuttering, input lag, and a generally terrible experience. Before you buy a new processor, learn the practical, software-level fixes that can relieve the pressure on your CPU.

The Problem with 100% CPU Usage

When you are playing a game, your processor (CPU) acts as the manager. It handles game logic, AI, physics, and tells the graphics card (GPU) what to draw. If your CPU hits 100% usage (on its active cores), it means the manager is overwhelmed. It can no longer give instructions to the GPU fast enough.

This state is known as a severe CPU bottleneck. The symptoms are very obvious:

  • Your average FPS might seem okay, but the game constantly stutters and freezes.
  • Your mouse and keyboard inputs feel delayed or "floaty."
  • Your GPU usage drops significantly (often below 70%) because the graphics card is spending all its time waiting for the CPU.
  • Audio might pop or cut out briefly during intense action.

If you check your PC usage using tools like Task Manager or MSI Afterburner and confirm your CPU is pegged near 100%, here are the most effective ways to fix it without immediately upgrading your hardware.

Solution 1: Cap Your Frame Rate (The Most Effective Fix)

This sounds counterintuitive, but capping your FPS is often the best solution for 100% CPU usage. If your GPU is capable of generating 120 FPS, but your CPU can only handle preparing 80 FPS, the CPU will kill itself trying to keep up, maxing out at 100% and causing stutter.

If you artificially cap your frame rate to 60 FPS, the CPU only has to prepare 60 frames per second. This immediately lowers the CPU's workload, reducing its usage from 100% down to a much more manageable 70-80%.

How to do it:

  • Look for an "FPS Limit" or "Max Frame Rate" setting in the game's display or graphics options.
  • If the game doesn't have one, use the Nvidia Control Panel (Manage 3D Settings > Max Frame Rate) or AMD Radeon Software (Radeon Chill) to enforce a driver-level cap.
  • RivaTuner Statistics Server (RTSS), which comes with MSI Afterburner, is widely considered the best tool for providing a perfectly smooth, stutter-free frame cap.

Solution 2: Increase the Graphics Resolution

Again, this sounds strange, but if you want to take the load off the CPU, you need to make the GPU work harder. If you are playing at 1080p and experiencing 100% CPU usage, the GPU is churning through frames too quickly.

By increasing the resolution to 1440p or 4K, you force the GPU to spend more time rendering each frame. This naturally slows down the overall frame rate, which gives the CPU more time to prepare the next frame, dropping CPU usage.

How to do it:

  • If you don't have a higher resolution monitor, use Nvidia DSR (Dynamic Super Resolution) or AMD VSR (Virtual Super Resolution) in your driver control panel. This allows the game to render at 1440p or 4K and downscale it to your 1080p monitor.
  • Turn the in-game resolution setting up.

Note: This will lower your overall FPS, but it will significantly reduce stuttering and make the gameplay feel much smoother.

Solution 3: Turn Down CPU-Heavy Graphics Settings

Most graphics settings only affect the GPU (like texture quality and anti-aliasing). However, a few specific settings hit the CPU incredibly hard. If your CPU is at 100%, turn these settings down immediately:

  • Crowd Density / Population: In games like Cyberpunk 2077 or GTA V, reducing the number of NPCs takes a massive load off the CPU's AI processing.
  • View Distance / Draw Distance: Lowering this means the CPU doesn't have to load and track objects that are far away.
  • Physics / Destruction: Complex particle physics and building destruction are heavily calculated by the CPU.
  • Ray Tracing: While Ray Tracing is primarily a GPU workload, it creates an enormous amount of "draw calls" that the CPU must process. Turning off Ray Tracing will dramatically lower CPU usage.

Solution 4: Kill Background Applications

If you have an older 4-core or 6-core processor (like an older Intel Core i5 or Ryzen 5), you do not have enough cores to run modern games and heavy background apps simultaneously. When a game demands 100% of your CPU, even a web browser updating in the background will cause a massive stutter.

How to do it:

  • Close all web browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox).
  • Close Discord (or disable Discord hardware acceleration).
  • Close game launchers you aren't actively using (Steam, Epic, EA, Ubisoft).
  • Disable aggressive antivirus scans while gaming.
  • Turn off unused RGB control software, which is notorious for using unnecessary CPU cycles.

Solution 5: Check for Thermal Throttling

Sometimes your CPU hits 100% usage not because the game is too demanding, but because the CPU has intentionally slowed itself down to prevent catching fire. This is called thermal throttling.

If your CPU cooler is clogged with dust, or the thermal paste has dried out, the CPU will reach 95-100°C. To survive, it will dramatically lower its clock speed (e.g., dropping from 4.5GHz to 2.0GHz). Because it is now running at half speed, a workload that normally takes 50% usage suddenly takes 100% usage.

How to check: Use a program like HWMonitor or MSI Afterburner to check CPU temperatures while gaming. If you are consistently above 90°C, you are likely thermal throttling.

The Fix: Clean your PC case, blow the dust out of your CPU cooler, ensure your case fans are working, and consider repasting your CPU.

Solution 6: Enable XMP / EXPO for Your RAM

While this is a RAM setting, it directly impacts CPU performance. The CPU needs to constantly pull data from system memory. If your RAM is running at slow default speeds (like 2133MHz) instead of its rated speed (like 3200MHz or 3600MHz), the CPU has to wait longer for data.

This waiting causes inefficiency, making the CPU struggle more than it should. Enabling XMP (Intel) or EXPO/DOCP (AMD) in your motherboard BIOS ensures your RAM runs at the correct speed, improving the CPU's ability to process data efficiently.

When Should You Actually Upgrade?

The solutions above are designed to "reduce the symptoms" of a weak CPU. They make the game playable by removing stutter, but they do this by limiting your maximum performance (capping FPS) or forcing the GPU to work harder (increasing resolution).

If you have applied these fixes and you still cannot maintain a playable frame rate (e.g., you are struggling to even hit 40-50 FPS at 1080p), it means the hardware limitation is simply too severe. At that point, upgrading the CPU (and potentially the motherboard and RAM) is the only real solution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my CPU at 100% while gaming?

Your CPU hits 100% when it cannot process game logic and draw calls fast enough to keep up with the GPU. This is known as a severe CPU bottleneck and often results in stuttering and input lag.

Does capping FPS lower CPU usage?

Yes. Capping your FPS prevents the GPU from demanding frames as fast as possible. This forces the CPU to prepare fewer frames per second, significantly reducing its workload and lowering its usage percentage.

Will upgrading my graphics card fix 100% CPU usage?

No. Upgrading your graphics card will actually make the problem worse. A faster GPU will demand frames even more quickly, placing even more stress on your already maxed-out processor.

Does lowering resolution fix CPU bottlenecks?

No. Lowering the resolution makes the game easier for the GPU, causing the frame rate to increase. A higher frame rate places a larger burden on the CPU, making the CPU bottleneck even worse.
